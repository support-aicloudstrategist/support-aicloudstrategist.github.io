What to evidence before changing contractor software or adding AI

1. Lead-source to estimator register

Capture phone calls, missed calls, website forms, WhatsApp, referrals, Google Business Profile clicks, architect/client emails and tender alerts with estimator, trade, urgency, location and next action.

2. Site-visit and quote follow-up queue

Separate new enquiry, booked site visit, quote promised, quote sent, revision requested, no response, won, lost and deferred work so follow-up does not live only inside an inbox.

3. Change-order and variation evidence map

Track who requested the change, photos/drawings, scope impact, pricing status, approval route, schedule impact and owner handoff before work proceeds or disputes emerge.

4. WhatsApp/photo/document controls

Map where site photos, voice notes, drawings, measurements, client approvals, purchase orders and subcontractor updates are stored, named and tied back to the job record.

5. Human-review boundaries for AI

Route contract terms, safety issues, structural questions, permit questions, disputes, payment promises, scope exclusions and uncertain measurements to responsible people, not AI-only replies.

6. Weekly owner dashboard

Show open quotes, aged follow-ups, site visits with no estimate, unapproved variations, blocked materials, unpaid milestones, dispute-risk jobs and owner-decisions due this week.

Use this as a proof-before-platform filter

If owners cannot see which enquiries, quotes, site visits, variations and customer approvals are still open, buying another CRM or project-management tool may move the leakage instead of fixing the operating evidence.

FAQ

Can this sit around existing contractor tools?

Yes. The checklist is designed to organize evidence around current phones, spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email, site photos, estimating software, CRM, field-service apps and project-management exports before replacing or extending systems.

What should not be automated without review?

Contract commitments, pricing exceptions, exclusions, safety concerns, permit questions, structural or engineering issues, disputes, payment promises and unclear measurements should route to a named human owner.
