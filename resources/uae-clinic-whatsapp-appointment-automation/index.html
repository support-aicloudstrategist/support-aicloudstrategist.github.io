UAE buyer questions AICS must answer before it deserves consideration

1. Can you see every enquiry route?

A credible UAE clinic growth asset should inventory WhatsApp Business, calls, web forms, Google Business Profile, Instagram DMs, referral partner messages and booking-platform requests. AICS positions this as an operational evidence map, not a claim that it replaces marketplaces, CRMs or front-desk teams.

2. Who owns the next action?

The owner dashboard should show whether each patient enquiry has an accountable staff owner, status, callback due time, booking handoff and escalation rule. This is the gap many clinics feel when more ad spend produces more chats but not more accountable follow-up.

3. What is safe for AI to do?

For UAE healthcare workflows, AICS should publish AI receptionist boundaries: no diagnosis, no treatment advice, no emergency triage claim, no DHA/MOH/DoH compliance certification and human escalation for clinical, pricing, complaint, consent or sensitive-data questions.

4. What proof exists today?

AICS has a clearly labelled simulated GCC clinic proof-of-method using synthetic enquiries only. It demonstrates the dashboard method without pretending to be a real UAE client result.

FAQ

Does AICS replace Okadoc, Doctify, Altibbi, Vezeeta or a healthcare CRM?

No. AICS is not a booking marketplace, telehealth platform, doctor directory or clinical CRM. It is an operating layer for enquiry capture, follow-up visibility and appointment ownership around existing tools.

Is this UAE healthcare legal, medical or DHA compliance advice?

No. This page is operational guidance only. Clinics should use qualified legal, compliance and medical advisers for formal decisions.

Why focus on WhatsApp before buying more clinic leads?

In many GCC clinics, WhatsApp is a high-intent patient channel. If staff cannot see who replied, who owns the follow-up, which patients were reminded and which conversations went unresolved, extra advertising can multiply waste.
