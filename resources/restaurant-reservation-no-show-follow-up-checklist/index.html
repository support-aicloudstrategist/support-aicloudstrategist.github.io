The ten checks before paying for more food-delivery ads, booking apps or random social posts

1. Missed call and WhatsApp capture

Separate missed reservation calls, WhatsApp enquiries, Google profile clicks, Instagram DMs, delivery-platform messages and website forms so demand is not hidden.

2. Booking confirmation

Record date, party size, time slot, dietary notes, deposit/prepay status where applicable and the confirmation channel.

3. No-show risk flags

Tag large groups, peak-hour reservations, unconfirmed bookings, repeated no-shows, late-night bookings and high-demand slots for extra confirmation steps.

4. Staff owner

Every reservation enquiry needs a named owner, backup owner and due time for confirmation or callback.

5. Waitlist recovery

Track who can fill a cancelled slot, what offer/message is safe, and when staff should stop contacting the waitlist.

6. Repeat-customer segments

Separate birthday/anniversary, weekday lunch, family dinner, corporate table, catering and high-frequency guests before sending generic offers.

7. Review prompt timing

Define when staff can request reviews, which experiences should be escalated internally first and how negative feedback is captured.

8. Consent and opt-out

Check appropriate consent wording, purpose limits, opt-out handling and local privacy/platform-policy review before automating repeat messages.

9. Closure reason

Use simple reasons such as booked, cancelled, no-show, no response, price/menu mismatch, full table, duplicate, complaint, refund issue or owner review.

10. Owner dashboard

Show new enquiries, confirmed bookings, unconfirmed bookings, no-shows, cancelled slots, waitlist fills, review prompts and repeat-customer follow-ups by source and owner.

Does every restaurant need a new booking app?

No. Some restaurants first need clearer missed-call capture, reservation owner assignment, no-show reasons, waitlist workflow, review prompt boundaries and repeat-customer segments.

Can this checklist be used globally?

Yes as an operational checklist, but messaging, consent, privacy, advertising, food-safety and platform rules vary by country and need qualified local review.
