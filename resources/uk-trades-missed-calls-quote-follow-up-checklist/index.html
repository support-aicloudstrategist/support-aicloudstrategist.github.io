The ten checks before buying more trade leads

1. Missed-call source

Record whether the missed call came from Google Business Profile, paid search, website, referral, van signage, Checkatrade-style listings, social media or repeat customers.

2. First-response SLA

Define who calls back within business hours, after hours, weekends and emergency job windows; do not rely on memory or voicemail alone.

3. Job-type qualification

Capture trade type, postcode, urgency, property type, photos, access constraints, budget band and whether the job is repair, install, maintenance or quote-only.

4. Quote ownership

Every quote needs an owner, sent date, amount band, next follow-up date, stale-quote status and loss reason.

5. WhatsApp and SMS evidence

Track opt-in, preferred channel, photo uploads, callback consent, opt-out handling and messages that need human judgement before automation.

6. Calendar and site visit handoff

Separate booked site visits, awaiting photos, awaiting landlord/tenant access, emergency visits and jobs needing parts or subcontractor checks.

7. Review and referral trigger

After completed work, route safe review requests and referral reminders through a controlled queue rather than ad-hoc staff messages.

8. Lost-job reasons

Use simple reasons: no answer, price, timing, outside area, competitor, not qualified, parts delay, customer paused or needs owner review.

9. Owner dashboard

Show new enquiries, missed calls, quotes due, site visits booked, follow-ups overdue, jobs won, jobs lost and unresolved messages by source.

10. Automation boundary

Escalate safety, gas/electrical, payment disputes, complaints, legal/privacy questions and unclear emergency requests to qualified humans.

FAQ

Should a trades firm buy more leads first or fix follow-up first?

If missed calls, stale quotes and unresolved WhatsApp messages are already visible, fix follow-up first so new lead spend is not wasted.

Is this only for one trade?

No. The checklist is written for plumbers, electricians, roofers, HVAC teams, builders, landscapers, cleaners and similar local home-service firms, with trade-specific safety and regulatory decisions escalated to qualified humans.
