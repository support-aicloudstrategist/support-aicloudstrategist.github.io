3. Ten leakage checks before buying another tool

  1. Referral source capture: every physician referral, portal request, fax, website form, phone call and ad enquiry has a source and timestamp.
  2. Owner assignment: every referral, prior-auth request and patient callback has one accountable owner, not a shared inbox assumption.
  3. Ageing queue: unresolved items are visible by 0–2 hours, same day, 1–2 days, 3–5 days and over 5 days.
  4. Prior-auth status clarity: requested, submitted, payer pending, missing info, patient pending, approved, denied, appealed and closed are distinct statuses.
  5. Patient access handoff: scheduling, eligibility, intake, benefits, clinical review and follow-up teams can see where work is stuck.
  6. Call abandonment recovery: missed calls and after-hours requests are matched to callback outcomes, not buried in phone logs.
  7. AI boundary review: bots or agents never answer diagnosis, emergency, treatment, payer guarantee, legal/privacy or clinical decision questions outside approved escalation paths.
  8. PHI minimisation: diagnostic evidence uses the minimum fields necessary, with qualified privacy/security review for any production data.
  9. Closed-loop referral proof: referring providers or internal teams can see whether the patient was reached, scheduled, pending or closed for a documented reason.
  10. Owner dashboard: leadership sees leakage by source, queue, owner, status, age and safe next action before approving platform spend.

5. Inbound diagnostic questions

  1. Which referral sources create requests today?
  2. Where do prior authorizations queue and who owns them?
  3. What percentage of work is ageing beyond same-day review?
  4. Which patient-access statuses are visible to the owner?
  5. What can be reviewed safely without unnecessary PHI?
  6. Which AI or staff responses require human escalation?
