Five checks before buying an AI receptionist

  1. Source: which channels and hours produced the aggregate signal?
  2. Owner: who is accountable for the callback, and who is the backup?
  3. Age: how long can an item wait before review or escalation?
  4. Status: can the team distinguish attempted, reached, scheduled and hold?
  5. Reversibility: what is the smallest safe workflow change to test without patient data?

Common questions

Why is my clinic not getting patients?

Do not infer a cause from a low booking count alone. First check aggregate enquiry sources, missed calls, callback ownership, response status and opening-hour routing for one defined time window.

What should a clinic check first when patient calls are missed?

Choose one time window, reconcile the aggregate call count, and assign a callback owner and backup before changing tools.

Should names, phone numbers or recordings be uploaded?

No. Start with aggregate counts, role ownership, callback status and review time. Hold when an input is unsafe or unclear.

Does a missed-call problem prove that a clinic needs an AI receptionist?

No. Separate volume, routing, staffing, opening-hours and follow-up ownership first. A tool decision comes only after the workflow and stop rules are visible.

What should a clinic check before calling an AI workflow DPDP compliant?

Do not treat this buyer aid as a compliance conclusion. Map purpose, data path, access roles, retention, vendor boundaries, notice/consent questions and incident ownership, then obtain qualified legal and privacy review before making a DPDP claim.
