Cloud and AI cost governance before you cut

Cuts without owners create reliability fights. Name who owns the spend first—then choose act, investigate, or hold.

Claim boundary

This note does not promise savings percentages, ROI, or bill reductions. It is buyer education on ownership before action.

When AWS, Azure, GCP, Kubernetes, or GPU lines spike, the first useful question is not “how much can we cut?” It is “who can defend this number and change it safely?”

Three buckets before any cut

  • Assigned — a product or team can own the next move.
  • Shared — needs an agreed split rule; averages are not ownership.
  • Unallocated — no trustworthy owner yet; hold or investigate, do not pretend.

AI and GPU spend follows the same rule

Production inference, shared training queues, and experimental clusters often share one “AI” line on the bill. Governance means naming idle rules and owners separately—not averaging them into a fake target.

What good looks like in the next meeting

Finance asks what changed. A named owner brings utilization evidence, pending releases, and a clear act / investigate / hold label. That is cost governance. Percent slogans are not.

FAQ: a cloud bill that suddenly looks wrong

Why is my cloud bill suddenly high? Confirm the provider, account, service and time window that changed, then map the material spend to an accountable owner before cutting or committing.

Should we cut the largest cloud line first? Not automatically. Separate assigned, shared and unallocated spend; the largest line may be shared or required, so classify evidence before choosing act, investigate or hold.

Who should own AI and GPU cloud spend? Name separate owners for production inference, shared training capacity and experiments, with an idle rule and a hold decision where ownership or evidence is missing.
