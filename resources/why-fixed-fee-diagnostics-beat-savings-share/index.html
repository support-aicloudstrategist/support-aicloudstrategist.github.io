Why fixed-fee diagnostics beat savings-share

When the advisor is paid on claimed savings, “hold” becomes expensive—and thin evidence gets dressed up as a cut.

Savings-share and contingent fee models look aligned with the buyer. In practice they tilt recommendations toward actions that are easy to monetize and away from the boring work that actually governs spend: ownership gaps, shared platforms and commitment risk.

Three distortions

  • Hold is punished. If the provider only gets paid when you cut, restraint looks like lost revenue.
  • Baselines get negotiated. Contested baselines and “what would have been spent” debates eat the engagement.
  • Ownership is optional. A savings register can look complete without naming who owns the next move.

What fixed fee buys

A written fixed fee after scope keeps the commercial boundary clear: you pay for a decision-ready dossier, not a percentage of hoped-for outcomes. Recommendations stay classified as act, investigate or hold. Implementation stays yours.
