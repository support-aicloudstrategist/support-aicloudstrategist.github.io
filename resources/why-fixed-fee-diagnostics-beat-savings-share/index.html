Why fixed-fee diagnostics beat savings-share

When the advisor is paid on claimed savings, “hold” becomes expensive—and thin evidence gets dressed up as a cut.

Savings-share and contingent fee models look aligned with the buyer. In practice they tilt recommendations toward actions that are easy to monetize and away from the boring work that actually governs spend: ownership gaps, shared platforms and commitment risk.

Three distortions

  • Hold is punished. If the provider only gets paid when you cut, restraint looks like lost revenue.
  • Baselines get negotiated. Contested baselines and “what would have been spent” debates eat the engagement.
  • Ownership is optional. A savings register can look complete without naming who owns the next move.

A worked contrast

Imagine a GPU pool that looks 30% idle overnight. A savings-share incentive pushes an immediate downsize recommendation. A fixed-fee diagnostic asks: is overnight idle intentional batch capacity, a missing autoscaling rule, or unallocated research spend? If evidence is thin, the honest output is investigate or hold—not a forced cut that breaks tomorrow’s training job and then gets argued as “savings.”

What fixed fee buys

A written fixed fee after scope keeps the commercial boundary clear: you pay for a decision-ready dossier, not a percentage of hoped-for outcomes. Recommendations stay classified as act, investigate or hold. Implementation stays yours. Finance can defend the fee on the invoice; engineering can reject unsafe cuts without negotiating the advisor’s paycheck.

Fixed fee also makes fit review honest. If the estate is a poor match—thin evidence rights, contested ownership or a mandate to guarantee savings—we can decline without leaving money on the table. That boundary protects both sides before any sensitive billing exports leave your tenancy.

Compare that to open-ended optimization retainers: hours accumulate, savings claims drift, and nobody can point to a dated act/investigate/hold register. The diagnostic ends with a dossier you own—so the engagement has a clear stop as well as a clear price.

FAQ

Does fixed fee mean no accountability? No. Accountability is the dossier quality: owners named, evidence confidence stated, and next moves dated—not a contingent percentage.

Can you still quantify opportunity? Yes, as ranges with confidence. What we refuse is guaranteeing savings or tying our fee to contested baselines.

When is savings-share especially risky? Multi-tenant platforms, Kubernetes shared services, and AI/GPU estates—where ownership is incomplete and “easy cuts” often hide reliability risk.

Illustrative example

Illustrative scenario—not a client case. A vendor proposes 25% of “identified savings” on a Kubernetes estate. Baseline uses last month’s peak. Mid-engagement, traffic drops and reserved capacity looks “wasteful.” Under savings-share, the advisor’s incentive is to cut nodes; under a fixed-fee diagnostic, the same evidence becomes investigate or hold until owners and SLO owners sign off. The fee stays the same either way—so the dossier can say “do not cut” without a commercial fight.
