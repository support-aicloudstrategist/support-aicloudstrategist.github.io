The seven checks before buying AI automation

1. Enquiry sources

List phone calls, missed calls, WhatsApp, email, forms, chat, booking links, ad leads, referrals and walk-ins. If sources are not visible, automation cannot prove what it recovered.

2. Owner and next action

Every lead, request, invoice, support item or follow-up should show an owner, next action, due date and unresolved flag before AI is allowed to move work forward.

3. Repeated manual work

Separate copy-paste, reminders, status chasing, document collection, reporting and routing from decisions that need a human owner.

4. Exceptions and risk

Mark urgent requests, complaints, medical/legal/financial questions, sensitive data, pricing exceptions, angry customers and uncertain AI outputs for human review.

5. CRM/spreadsheet hygiene

Check duplicate contacts, missing source, stale status, blank follow-up date, missing consent/opt-out notes and lost-reason gaps before connecting more tools.

6. Owner dashboard

Decide what the owner needs weekly: open enquiries, response SLA misses, stuck tasks, handoff gaps, no-response list, risk queue and automation backlog.

7. Pilot boundary

Start with a fixed-scope diagnostic and one safe workflow. Avoid promising full transformation, headcount reduction, revenue guarantee or compliance status.

FAQ

Does this mean every small business needs AI?

No. Some businesses need cleaner forms, better CRM fields, a callback rule or a dashboard before AI.

Can this checklist be used globally?

Yes as an operating-readiness checklist, but privacy, employment, industry, medical, legal, financial and advertising rules vary by region and need qualified review where relevant.
