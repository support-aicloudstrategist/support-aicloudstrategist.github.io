What buyers should not share in a first review

No credentials or secrets

Do not share passwords, OTPs, API keys, access tokens, SSH keys, VPN profiles, admin invites, cloud-console roles, database credentials, EHR logins, WhatsApp Business tokens, payment credentials or privileged mailbox access.

No patient-identifiable data

Do not share names, phone numbers, Emirates IDs, passport details, MRNs, clinical notes, lab reports, images, prescriptions, diagnoses, payment records, claims, audio recordings, chat transcripts or patient documents.

No regulator-sensitive material by default

Do not upload regulator correspondence, incident reports, unresolved complaints, audit letters, legal advice, DPO opinions, breach notices or health-information-exchange material unless a separate approved scope and qualified advisers permit it.

No unsupported external claims

Do not claim compliance, certification, partnership, patient outcome, appointment growth, savings, ROI, AI accuracy, approval by DHA/DoH/MOHAP/Malaffi/NABIDH or procurement success unless independently evidenced and approved.

FAQ

Can AICS review live systems during first contact?

No. The first review should use redacted descriptions, owner lists, non-sensitive screenshots and summary exports only. Live access belongs in a separate approved scope with defined owners and security controls.

Can this be used by a clinic owner before buying WhatsApp or AI receptionist software?

Yes. It helps the owner identify what must be handled by humans, advisers or accountable internal owners before automating follow-up.

Is measured buyer demand proven?

No. This asset improves readiness, search coverage and trust posture. Measured demand requires analytics, search-console, lead, inbox or CRM evidence.
