Evidence checklist before buying another platform or automation tool

1. Source-to-owner freight enquiry register

Capture website forms, calls, email, WhatsApp, portals, directories, agent referrals and existing shipper requests. Every row needs source, owner, mode, lane, cargo type, priority, age and next action.

2. Quote follow-up and aged request queue

Track due date, pricing owner, operations handoff, follow-up timestamp, won/lost/blocked reason and no-response status so quote work does not disappear between sales, pricing and operations.

3. Missing-document blocker board

Separate commercial invoice, packing list, KYC, HS code, permits, MSDS, insurance, PO, booking confirmation and delivery-address blockers from general shipment status updates.

4. Shipment exception ageing queue

Classify pickup issues, warehouse holds, carrier delays, customs queries, payment holds, address issues, damage notes, demurrage or detention risk and delivery appointment issues by age and reviewer.

5. Customer update evidence log

Record channel, template, owner, last-sent time and promised next update. The goal is proof of what was communicated, not an unsupported claim about fewer delays.

6. Human-review boundary route

Flag customs, dangerous goods, pricing, claims, insurance, demurrage, detention, delay notices, payment disputes and contractual commitments for named human review before automation or AI suggestions.

FAQ

Can a freight forwarder use this checklist without changing systems?

Yes. The first step is an evidence map using existing call logs, shared inboxes, WhatsApp exports where appropriate, quote sheets, TMS status reports, customer portal records and owner/operator interviews.

Does AICS replace TMS, WMS, customs broker or customer-portal software?

No. AICS should sit around those tools as the evidence, workflow and owner-dashboard layer when the logistics business needs to prove what is leaking before implementing more automation.
