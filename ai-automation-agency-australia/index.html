Where Australian SMEs usually leak revenue

Website, webchat and form enquiries

Prospects ask for pricing, availability, appointments or documents, but there is no owner-visible status, second follow-up or lost-reason capture.

Missed calls and after-hours callbacks

Phone leads arrive during treatments, site visits, classes, meetings or after-hours. A callback queue and response SLA can be more valuable than buying more ads.

CRM-lite follow-up

Many small teams do not need enterprise complexity first. AICS can start with a simple sheet/CRM pipeline, status fields, reminders and weekly owner dashboard.

Appointment and quote booking

Forms, calls, chat messages and booking links need one workflow for slots, quote requests, reminders, no-show follow-up and human escalation.

Privacy Act and APP-aware consent prompts

Healthcare, education, finance and local services should add purpose, consent, opt-out and data-handling discipline. This is operational readiness guidance, not legal advice.

AI chatbot and voice-agent triage

Use AI to capture context and route next steps, not to make unsupported medical, legal, financial, compliance or revenue promises.

What a practical Australia automation sprint includes

  1. Leakage audit: inspect public website, forms, phone CTA, webchat, booking link and follow-up journey.
  2. Workflow map: define trigger, owner, next step, deadline, message template, escalation and evidence field.
  3. Build path: choose simple tools first: forms, sheets, CRM, calendar, email, dashboard or supported automation platform.
  4. Trust boundary: add consent, opt-out, no-sensitive-advice, human escalation and claim limits where needed.
  5. Owner dashboard: show enquiry count, response status, unresolved leads, missed calls, follow-up due and lost reasons.
