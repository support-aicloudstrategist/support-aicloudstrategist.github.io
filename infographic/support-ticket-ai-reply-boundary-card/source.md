## Seven checks before AI sends or drafts support replies

- **1. Classify the ticket risk:** Owner question: Is this a routine question, refund dispute, outage, access problem, data request, bug, complaint or legal/compliance issue? Safe first action: Let AI draft only low-risk replies; route risky tickets to the named human owner.
- **2. Verify the source of truth:** Owner question: Which help article, policy, order record, incident note or product owner update supports the reply? Safe first action: Do not answer from memory or model confidence when the current source is missing.
- **3. Protect sensitive data:** Owner question: Could the reply expose credentials, payment data, health/legal details, customer records, logs or another customer’s information? Safe first action: Mask sensitive details and ask for secure-channel handling before sharing records.
- **4. Separate apology from promise:** Owner question: Does the wording promise a refund, fix date, SLA credit, legal position, security outcome or future feature? Safe first action: Use bounded status language and require owner approval for commitments.
- **5. Add escalation stop rules:** Owner question: What words or conditions force handoff: angry customer, chargeback, outage, repeated failure, data deletion, account lockout or regulated request? Safe first action: Make stop rules visible inside the helpdesk workflow before automation is enabled.
- **6. Record human approval:** Owner question: Who reviewed the reply pattern, when, and what evidence link shows approval? Safe first action: Keep an approval register for templates, macros, AI prompts and high-risk examples.
- **7. Review failed replies weekly:** Owner question: Which AI drafts were edited, rejected, escalated or caused follow-up confusion? Safe first action: Feed review findings into updated templates, source articles and routing rules.

**Truth boundary:** Educational operations guide only — not legal, compliance, security, support-SLA, customer-result, revenue, savings, refund, incident-resolution, ranking, certification, or guaranteed-performance advice.
