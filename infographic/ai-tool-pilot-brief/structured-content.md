# Before You Buy an AI Tool: A 6-Box Pilot Brief

## Overview
Use this educational brief to define a small, reviewable pilot before choosing an AI tool.

## Learning Objectives
The viewer will understand:
1. The six areas to define before starting an AI-tool pilot.
2. The minimum pilot record needed for an evidence-based review.
3. The proof boundary for this educational material.

---

## Section 1: Business task

**Key Concept**: Define one bounded task and its owner.

**Content**:
- Name one task, its current owner, the trigger that starts it and the output that marks it complete.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn clipboard icon
- Subject: task flowing from trigger to output with an owner badge
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "1 · BUSINESS TASK"
- Labels: "TASK", "OWNER", "TRIGGER", "OUTPUT"

---

## Section 2: Evidence baseline

**Key Concept**: Establish a consistent starting record.

**Content**:
- Record current time, error, rework or queue evidence using a consistent method. If no baseline exists, observe first.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn ruler and notebook
- Subject: baseline marks and observation note
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "2 · EVIDENCE BASELINE"
- Labels: "TIME", "ERROR", "REWORK", "QUEUE", "OBSERVE FIRST"

---

## Section 3: Data boundary

**Key Concept**: State what data is and is not allowed.

**Content**:
- List the data the pilot may use, the data it must not use and the approved storage or access path.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn boundary box
- Subject: allowed folder, restricted folder and approved path
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "3 · DATA BOUNDARY"
- Labels: "ALLOWED", "RESTRICTED", "APPROVED PATH"

---

## Section 4: Human control

**Key Concept**: Keep accountable review and stop points visible.

**Content**:
- Identify who reviews outputs, what requires approval and when the workflow must stop or return to a person.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn person, check mark and stop sign
- Subject: review, approval and human return path
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "4 · HUMAN CONTROL"
- Labels: "REVIEW", "APPROVAL", "STOP", "RETURN TO PERSON"

---

## Section 5: Pilot measure

**Key Concept**: Use one process measure and one quality check.

**Content**:
- Choose one observable process measure and one quality check. Define how both will be recorded.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn gauge and checklist
- Subject: measure paired with quality check
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "5 · PILOT MEASURE"
- Labels: "PROCESS MEASURE", "QUALITY CHECK", "RECORD BOTH"

---

## Section 6: Exit decision

**Key Concept**: Finish with an explicit evidence-backed decision.

**Content**:
- At the review date, choose: proceed, revise or stop. Record the evidence and owner for the next decision.

**Visual Element**:
- Type: hand-drawn three-way signpost
- Subject: proceed, revise or stop
- Treatment: simple paper-cut card

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "6 · EXIT DECISION"
- Labels: "PROCEED", "REVISE", "STOP", "EVIDENCE + OWNER"
