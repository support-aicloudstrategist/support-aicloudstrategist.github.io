# Structured content — Clinic Intake AI Safety Card: 7 Checks Before Automating Patient Questions

## Learning objective
Help clinic owners inspect patient-intake automation boundaries safely before increasing public-facing automation.

## Checks
- **1. Define allowed questions:** Owner question: Which intake questions are administrative only, such as location, hours, appointment type, documents, or callback preference? Safe first action: Keep medical symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, urgency, and medication questions routed to humans.
- **2. Show the handoff rule:** Owner question: When must the AI stop and send the person to staff or emergency guidance already approved by the clinic? Safe first action: Write a visible handoff rule before connecting the intake tool to patients.
- **3. Protect sensitive details:** Owner question: What personal or health details should not be typed into a public chat or unmanaged form? Safe first action: Collect the minimum needed and avoid free-text medical history in low-control channels.
- **4. Use approved wording:** Owner question: Has the clinic owner reviewed the exact phrases patients will see? Safe first action: Use simple scripted wording and avoid advice, reassurance, diagnosis, or promise language.
- **5. Assign a response owner:** Owner question: Who reads escalated messages, missed calls, incomplete forms, and confused replies each business day? Safe first action: Name a staff owner before increasing intake automation volume.
- **6. Test edge cases manually:** Owner question: What happens for urgent symptoms, angry patients, minors, billing concerns, language gaps, or repeated messages? Safe first action: Run a small internal test log and fix unsafe flows before public use.
- **7. Review weekly evidence:** Owner question: Which questions repeated, which handoffs worked, and where did patients still need help? Safe first action: Track themes, handoffs, unanswered items, and owner notes before expanding automation.

## Boundary
Educational operations guide only — not medical, legal, privacy, compliance, diagnosis, treatment, certification, savings, ranking, customer-result, approval, or guaranteed-performance advice.
