# AI Automation for Small Business: Owner Checklist Before Buying a Tool

A no-credentials, synthetic checklist covering trigger, owner, rules, exceptions and the smallest evidence-matched next move.

1. What repeatable job is being considered? Example answer: “When a web enquiry arrives someone copies it into a sheet”. Next move: Define trigger and output. Boundary: Use synthetic example only.
2. Who owns the output? Example answer: “Operations lead reviews the queue”. Next move: Name accountable owner. Boundary: No personal data.
3. Which steps are rules? Example answer: “Assign by service type; escalate unknowns”. Next move: Separate judgement. Boundary: Human review for exceptions.
4. What stops the workflow? Example answer: “Missing details or sensitive request”. Next move: Write stop and escalation path. Boundary: No regulated or payment data.
5. What is the smallest next move? Example answer: “Test a checklist with synthetic rows”. Next move: Run reversible experiment. Boundary: No ROI or savings promise.

Decision rule: A small business should define the repeatable job, accountable owner, rule-versus-judgement steps, exception stop path and reversible evidence-matched next move before selecting AI automation.

Claim boundary: Not a client case study, productivity guarantee, savings claim, security assessment, legal/compliance advice, ranking, demand or customer outcome.
