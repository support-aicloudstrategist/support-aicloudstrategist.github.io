A useful starting point is not “which tool can automate this?” but “which task is safe enough to hand to AI?”

I would run a simple AI task intake gate first:

1. What is the exact repeatable task?
Name the work item clearly before choosing any tool.

2. Who owns the output?
A named human should be able to approve, correct, or stop the workflow.

3. What source is allowed?
Use approved documents, examples, or records instead of unsupported guessing.

4. What can go wrong?
Mark privacy, promise, payment, legal, medical, compliance, or brand risks before automation.

5. Where does the workflow pause?
Define the handoff point for uncertain, sensitive, or destructive actions.

6. What evidence is kept?
Log the source, draft, reviewer, decision, correction, and timestamp.

Good early candidates are internal summaries, checklist preparation, categorisation, meeting-note cleanup, and first-pass research outlines. Customer-facing replies, pricing commitments, contract language, payments, credentials, legal/compliance/medical interpretation, and destructive system changes should require human review or stay outside automation.
