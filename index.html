When the diagnostic becomes relevant

Spend is visible. Accountability is not.

Cloud and AI costs become difficult to govern when billing, architecture, product value and ownership are held in different systems—or by different teams.

01

Rising cost, unclear cause

Monthly cloud, model or GPU spend changes faster than finance and engineering can explain.

02

Unallocated consumption

Shared platforms, clusters, inference and data costs cannot be connected confidently to a product or owner.

03

Conflicting views

Finance, platform and product teams use different baselines, definitions and decision criteria.

04

Unsafe optimization pressure

A cost recommendation exists, but the reliability, security, workload or commercial consequences are unresolved.

From invoice line to decision owner

Build one traceable view of cost, context and control.

The diagnostic does not label an increase as waste from a billing chart alone. It connects the available evidence to the business and technical context required for a safe decision.

Ideal fit

Designed for teams with material spend and incomplete cost ownership.

The diagnostic is most relevant when cloud or AI consumption has become strategically important but the organization does not yet have one trusted view of cost, context and authority.

Strong fit

  • Cloud-native SaaS, digital-product or AI-enabled operations
  • Multiple accounts, clusters, products, environments or owners
  • Growing model, inference, GPU or data-platform consumption
  • Budget, margin, renewal or scaling decision approaching
  • Finance and technology views do not reconcile cleanly

May not fit

  • Spend is too small to justify a structured diagnostic
  • A mature FinOps function already has accepted allocation and controls
  • The request is for guaranteed savings or contingent-fee optimization
  • The primary need is implementation rather than decision evidence
  • No accountable stakeholder can approve scope or actions

Scope boundaries

The diagnostic informs decisions. It does not take control.

Scope, access, evidence and authority remain explicit throughout the engagement.

Included where relevant

Cloud accounts and subscriptions, Kubernetes, GPUs, model and inference usage, data-platform costs, commitments, allocation, ownership and governance controls.

No production changes

No shutdown, rightsizing, commitment purchase, architecture change or policy enforcement is executed as part of the diagnostic.

No guaranteed savings

No fixed ROI, savings percentage, universal benchmark or realized-value claim is promised.

Not a formal audit

The engagement is not an accounting opinion, certification, regulatory assessment or substitute for provider support.

Buyer questions

Important boundaries before requesting a review.

Is the fit review free?

Yes. The fit review is a short suitability and scoping conversation at no charge. It is not the paid diagnostic and does not include cost findings from confidential billing material.

What does the diagnostic cost?

Typical single-estate diagnostics fall in USD 2,500–3,000 (India: INR 1,00,000–1,50,000). Complex multi-cloud or GPU estates usually fall between USD 5,500–12,000. The first five paid diagnostics receive a time-boxed 20% founding credit. The fee is fixed in writing after scope.

Do you help with AWS, Azure, GCP, Kubernetes or GPU cost optimization?

Yes—when those estates are in scope. Start with AWS cost optimization, Azure cost optimization, GCP cost savings, Kubernetes cost management or GPU and AI cost optimization, then book a fit review.

Is this FinOps consulting or a cloud cost audit?

It is a fixed-fee FinOps consulting diagnostic and bounded cloud cost audit for decision evidence—not a savings-share program, platform license or statutory financial audit.

Does AICS require production credentials?

Not through the public website. If an engagement proceeds, the evidence and access model is agreed before data transfer. Export-based or client-controlled approaches may be appropriate.

Does the diagnostic guarantee savings?

No. It identifies evidence-backed decisions, uncertainties and safe next actions. Actual outcomes depend on client approval, implementation, measurement and operating conditions.

Must a company already use AI?

No. Cloud cost ownership can remain the core scope. AI, GPU, model or inference costs are included only where they are material and supported by relevant evidence.

Does this replace a FinOps platform?

No. Platforms can provide billing, allocation and optimization signals. The diagnostic focuses on ownership, evidence, constraints and the decisions those signals support.
