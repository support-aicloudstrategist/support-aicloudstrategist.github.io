Decision trigger

Spend is rising faster than accountable value.

This review is for founders, finance partners, engineering leaders, platform owners and product teams who can see cloud or AI cost increasing but cannot explain it by workload, product, customer, model or owner. The goal is an action-ready economic baseline, not a generic savings percentage.

The review starts with billing and usage exports, workload maps, commercial assumptions and owner interviews supplied through an agreed secure route. It does not require production credentials through the public website.

Four economics questions

  1. What is being consumed?
  2. Who owns the decision?
  3. What unit of value should cost follow?
  4. Which action is safe now?
Method

From invoice lines to owned decisions.

1. Establish the baseline

Normalize the relevant cloud, model, GPU, data and platform cost into a dated scope. Separate recurring consumption, committed spend, idle capacity, transfer, storage and third-party AI charges.

2. Map cost to owners and value

Connect major cost pools to a workload, product, environment, business owner and useful value unit where evidence permits. Unallocated spend remains visible rather than being forced into a misleading model.

3. Test action choices

Compare rightsizing, scheduling, architecture, model choice, caching, retention, commitment and product-policy actions. Each action records expected benefit, risk, dependency, owner and verification method.

4. Create the control loop

Define thresholds, anomaly ownership, approval gates and review cadence so the first saving does not disappear after the report. Forecasts are labelled assumptions until observed.

What this review does not claim.

It does not guarantee a saving percentage, replace cloud-provider support, execute production shutdowns, purchase commitments or certify financial statements. Results depend on the quality of billing exports, ownership data, architecture context and the team's authority to implement actions.

Teams with mature allocation, active FinOps governance and reliable unit economics may need a targeted engineering engagement rather than this baseline review.

Buyer questions

Questions the economics review is designed to answer.

Why did cloud or AI cost increase?

The review separates growth caused by useful demand from idle capacity, architecture choices, model routing, data retention, transfer, experimentation and unallocated ownership. A cost increase is not labelled waste until the workload and value context are understood.

Which cost action is safe first?

Candidate actions are ranked by evidence, expected value, implementation effort, service risk, data risk and reversibility. Shutting down or committing spend solely from a billing chart is excluded. The responsible workload owner must accept the action and verification rule.

How should AI unit economics be measured?

The useful unit depends on the product: a completed workflow, evaluated answer, resolved case, active customer or another business event. The model documents the numerator, denominator, exclusions and data gaps so finance and engineering use the same definition.

Can this replace a FinOps platform?

No. A platform may supply allocation, anomaly and commitment data. The review is useful when owners still need to choose the decision model, evidence boundary and action sequence. Mature teams may move directly to targeted engineering or platform operations.
